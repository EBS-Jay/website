The Owner’s Boardroom

Who do you talk to when the toughest decisions are yours?

The Owner’s Boardroom is a small private group of established business owners facilitated by Jay Grosman. We’re launching with our founding St. Louis chapter. Members meet online each month and also get together in person. Every owner gets time to bring a real challenge, hear how other owners would approach it, and decide what to do next.

A room where you can be real

You Can’t Talk About Everything With Your Team.

The employee issue. The cash-flow worry. The decision you keep second-guessing. You need a room where you can say what’s actually going on.

Once a month, step out of the day-to-day with a small group of business owners who understand what it takes to run a company.

Bring the problem you’ve been carrying. Leave with perspective, a next step, and people who will ask how it went.

Membership by application

Who’s a good fit?

You’ve run a service business for at least two years. You make decisions for the business and are willing to share what you’ve learned with other owners.

We consider how each owner will contribute and whether the group can help with the decisions they face.

Each chapter accepts only one business per industry. That gives owners room to talk openly about challenges without a direct competitor at the table.

Applying does not guarantee a seat. We select members with care because the people in the room shape the value of every conversation.

Practical answers before we talk

Your Questions, Answered.

How often does the Owner’s Boardroom meet?

The group meets online once a month. Members of the St. Louis chapter also get together in person.

Can anyone join the Owner’s Boardroom?

Membership requires an application, an interview, and acceptance by the Elevating Business Solutions team. Jay Grosman facilitates the meetings.

Can two businesses in the same industry join the same chapter?

Each chapter accepts only one business per industry.

How is this different from networking?

The focus is working through real business challenges, decisions, and commitments. Meetings are not built around sales pitches or exchanging referrals.
